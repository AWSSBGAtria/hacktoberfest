Participant Handbook Before you arrive
Step zero

Before you arrive.

This event is open to all university and college students, whether you are a beginner writing your first lines of code or an experienced open-source contributor. There is no registration fee — you get the hack day, workshops, mentor guidance, refreshments and swags for free.

Who can attend

All university and college students. Beginners and experienced contributors are both welcome.

Team size

Solo or in teams of up to 4 members. No team yet? Find one during the morning team formation session.

What it costs

Nothing. No fee, and food and attendee kits are covered while supplies last.

Be there before 8:15 AM

Registration opens at 8:30 AM, so arrive at the venue before 8:15 AM. You need time to check in and be seated before the inauguration at 9:30.

Teams check in together

If you are building as a team, every member must be present at the desk at the same time. Check in as a group — we cannot admit a team member-wise.

Your wristband

Every participant gets a wristband at check-in. It is the only proof you are part of this Hack Day.

Keep your wristband on

Wear it for the entire day. It is how we identify participants, and without it you will not be considered for any activity during the Hack Day — no prizes, no food, no recognition. If it falls off or breaks, get it replaced at the registration desk straight away.

Attendee checklist — tick these off
  • Laptop & Charger (mandatory for hands-on hacking)
  • Valid College / University Student ID Card
  • Active GitHub account (ready to submit PRs)
  • Joined the official WhatsApp group (for live event updates)
  • Git installed on your laptop (we recommend this beforehand)
  • Registration completed on MLH (required for the Attendee Kit)

The day itself.

Be at the venue before 8:15 AM. Registration opens at 8:30 AM for check-in, and the day runs through the awards at 7:00 PM. Mentors and organizers will be on deck throughout to support every builder.

08:30 – 09:30 Check-in & RegistrationArrive before 8:15 AM. Teams check in together at one desk, and every participant is issued a wristband — keep it on for the rest of the day.
09:30 – 10:00 Inauguration, Opening Ceremony & Hack Day BriefingWelcome from the AWS Student Builder Group and Atria faculty, then the tracks, rules, and judging criteria.
10:00 – 05:00 Hack Day: Hacking & BuildingThe main build window — pick a track, claim issues, and pair up with mentors on the floor. The quiz and meal breaks below fall inside it.
11:30 – 11:45 Quiz 1A quick developer trivia break between commits — fifteen minutes, teams of up to four.
12:45 – 01:30 Lunch BreakLunch is provided on campus. Step away, refuel, and swap project ideas with the room.
03:00 – 03:15 Quiz 2Second round of trivia before the final push — same rules, harder questions.
05:00 – 05:30 Snacks, Refreshments & NetworkingTea, coffee, and snacks — plus open networking with mentors, peers, and organizers before judging.
05:30 – 06:30 Judging & Project EvaluationMentors and judges review demos, pull requests, and project write-ups against the track criteria.
06:30 – 07:00 Awards & Closing CeremonyWinners announced, prizes and swags handed out, and a group photo before we wrap.
05:00 PM · Submissions open

Project submissions go through the MLH challenges page, which opens at 5:00 PM. That is when teams start submitting — there is no other way in.

05:30 PM · Judging starts

Judging begins at 5:30 PM sharp. A project that is not submitted by then cannot be evaluated, so submit before the window closes.

Submitting well

Leave time for it: a public repo, a clear write-up, and a demo you can actually run. Lifting the public repo is not a submission.

Challenge tracks

Three ways to build.

Every project can qualify for the core Hacktoberfest challenge, and any project that also uses Gemma 4 or AWS can qualify for those too. One project can take more than one track. Mentors are on-site to help.

Step 01

Pick a track

Core Hacktoberfest, Gemma 4, or AWS. One project can qualify for more than one.

Step 02

Build all day

Mentors from AWS Student Builder Group and open source roam the room from 10:15 AM.

Step 03

Demo and submit

Push your repo, open your PR, and get your project on stage.

Core Hacktoberfest Challenge

Best Open-Source AI Project

The main Hacktoberfest track, run at every Hack Day. Build an original project where open-source or open-weight AI does real work: an agent skill, an open-weight model, or a model harness. These are examples within one challenge, and you may combine them.

  • Open-source or open-weight AI must be an important part of the project.
  • Published in a public GitHub repository, under an open-source license.
  • An agent skill must comply with the Agent Skill Open Standard.
  • A model-harness entry needs an original implementation, or meaningful changes to an existing open-source harness.

The winning team is announced on stage; prizes are confirmed closer to the day.

Google Partner Challenge

Best Use of Gemma 4

Build with Gemma 4, Google's lightweight open-weights multimodal model, through the Gemini API. Text and images together, or a focused tool, or a fast prototype — your call.

  • Multimodal experience — work with text and images to build an assistant that understands more of what users share.
  • Focused AI tools — a tool for learning, creativity, productivity, or your community.
  • Rapid prototyping — move quickly from an idea to a working prototype using an open model through the Gemini API.

The winning team is announced on stage; prizes are confirmed closer to the day.

AWS Student Builder Group Special

Best Use of AWS

Our own bonus track for this Hack Day. Use the AWS ecosystem to turn an idea into something real and scalable. Any AWS service counts — pick whichever fits your project, and combining several is encouraged.

  • Cloud-powered solutions — applications that are scalable, reliable, and ready for real-world use.
  • Innovative use of AWS — combine services creatively to solve a meaningful problem in your community, on campus, or beyond.
  • Build and deploy — go beyond a prototype by deploying on AWS and showing how the cloud powers your solution.

AWS services must be an important part of the project, with a clear explanation of how they contribute. The strongest AWS-powered build takes the track; prizes are confirmed closer to the day.

Team up beforehand, or meet someone during the morning and join their team. Solo builders are just as welcome. All experience levels welcome.

Food & FAQ.

12:45 – 01:30 PM

Lunch

On campus. Hand in your food coupon at the counter to collect it.

05:00 – 05:30 PM

Snacks & refreshments

Tea, coffee, and snacks — hand in your snack coupon at the counter.

One serving each. This is strict.

Each participant gets one food coupon and one snack coupon, redeemable only at the matching break. Hand the coupon in at the counter — and that is your serving. There are no second servings, and asking for one will mean going without. Please do not queue twice.

Q1Who can attend this Hack Day?

All university and college students — beginners writing their first lines of code and experienced contributors alike.

Q2Is there any registration fee?

No! The hack day, workshops, mentor guidance, food and swags are all free.

Q3What should I bring to the event?

Your laptop, charger, student ID, and a valid GitHub account. Install Git beforehand if you can.

Q4Can I participate individually or do I need a team?

You can participate solo or in teams of up to 4 members. If you don't have a team yet, you can easily find teammates during the morning team formation session.

Q5I am a complete beginner to Open Source. Can I still join?

Absolutely. Dedicated mentors will guide you through Git commands, finding good first issues, and submitting clean pull requests.

Q6How does the MLH registration work?

Use any 'Register Now' button to reach the official MLH event page. Log in or create an account, answer the questions, and join the WhatsApp group for live announcements.

Q7When do I need to arrive, and how does check-in work?

Be at the venue before 8:15 AM; registration opens at 8:30 AM. If you are in a team, every member must be at the desk together — we cannot check people in one at a time.

Q8Why do I need a wristband?

It is how we identify you as a participant. Without it you will not be considered for any activity, including prizes and food. If it comes off, get a replacement at the desk.
